May 13, 2026

Entry 003 — A Day Away From Code

Different activities going on today. I decided to give a break to coding and instead do some research on courses that I could maybe sing up for. All AI related. Honestly, I don't want to spend money so I was just looking at free ones. Found one on the AI in education page. Did it in an hour. The course was written in 2024 and focused on ChatGPT only. I wouldn't say it was pointless but it was definitely not what I was looking for.

After I did that I went all in on LinkedIn. I haven't used it in seven years but apparently it's the way to go now. Give yourself visibility on LinkedIn and suddenly you're someone. I started following a ton of people and mostly looking for profiles who are related to education and AI. So far, it's working. I found out about two different webinars I think could be interesting. I already feel like I'm starting to be part of this world (delusional I know).

Btw, I had to update my LinkedIn picture… nightmare. I mean, when do you ever take a picture of yourself smiling, no sunglasses, formal clothes, nice background. Not my usual Wednesday. I miss working in the US and having a picture day. Anyway I had my boyfriend take a thousand pictures of me the other day of which I only liked one. I sent it to ChatGPT and asked it to change the background and make it LinkedIn perfect. Somehow it made my head huge and changed my outfit and whole body. After a few tweaks, perfect. It didn't turn out so bad.

And that's it for today. More tomorrow!

Entry 002 — Updating Claude

I've been stuggling to update Claude for 15 minutes. I knew Claude Code wasn't getting auto updated on my computer but I didn't know how to fix it. I tried to do it last week but I just gave up.

Today I decided to try again. It wasn't working, I had to type so many things in my terminal and ultimately I'm just scared of typing on my computer terminal without using a folder in case AI takes over my whole computer.

15 minutes later I got it done, no issues (that I know of :))

Today I was teaching a student and tried Playlish with him. Great way to spot improvements. I'm trying to implement a feature where the game gives feedback on mistakes and also saves information about the user and creates a database with usual errors they make.

Chatting to Claude about it. I thought I wanted feedback after each incorrect response but after discussing it with Claude (I know, crazy) I realized it's better not to flood kids with boring feedback and let them have fun and learn.

I've realized that asking Claude to ask me questions helps me figure out what I actually want to do. I fixed the feedback feature and went on to the next.

Whenever a game ended, the user still saw the next button and the message that said the game was over appeared only under the game. It was really confusing. I told Claude and after a few questions, fixed. I don't really know what it did but it works now!

May 11th 2026

Entry 001 — Starting this site

I've been using AI tools for about a year now — mostly Claude, chatGPT, Gemini, some NotebookLM — and I kept thinking someone should document what it actually looks like to learn this as a teacher, not a developer. So I'm doing it myself.

This site is the first step. I built it with help from Claude, pushed it to GitHub, and figured out GitHub Pages. It took longer than I expected and less time than I feared. I am now writing this from the github editor and trying not to screw up.

What I want this journal to be: honest notes about the process. What I tried, what confused me, what clicked. No performance, no pretending I knew what I was doing.

More entries coming. This is entry one.
