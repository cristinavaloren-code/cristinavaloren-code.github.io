Currently building 2026–Present

Oppy

What started as an experiment to make lesson planning easier has turned into an actual EdTech product. Oppy is an AI-powered platform that helps aspiring primary school teachers in Spain prepare their teaching programmes, develop teaching units and navigate educational legislation.

I built it using Claude Code, learning pretty much everything along the way. From figuring out why the AI couldn't find information to dealing with enormous PDFs, unexpected API costs and features that looked great until someone actually tried them.

The best part? People are actually using it. And having real users has completely changed the way I think about building things. It's no longer just about whether I CAN make something work. It's about whether it actually helps someone.

Still developing. Still learning. Still breaking things occasionally.

TeachUnit

One of my early attempts at using AI to make teaching and curriculum planning easier. I wanted to create something that could help teachers organise lessons, educational content and teaching programmes without spending hours on everything.

It also made me question how useful an AI-generated lesson plan really is when actual classrooms rarely follow the plan. That question eventually helped me rethink who I wanted to build for.

On hold May 2026

Playlish

I had a tutoring student who couldn't sit still long enough to learn irregular verbs. So I built him a game.

Playlish is a gamified English-learning website for teenagers, designed around the idea that engagement is not optional. It's the whole point.

It was also one of my first opportunities to experiment with AI-assisted development and discover how much you learn when someone actually uses what you've built.
